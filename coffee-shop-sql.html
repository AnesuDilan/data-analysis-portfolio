SQL Case Study

Coffee Shop Sales Analysis

An end-to-end SQL analysis project developed using Microsoft SQL Server to transform raw coffee shop sales data into actionable business insights. The project demonstrates data cleaning, exploratory data analysis, business querying and performance analysis using advanced SQL techniques.

Business Problem

Coffee shops generate thousands of daily transactions across multiple locations, products and trading hours. While this data contains valuable business information, it can be difficult to identify sales trends, high-performing products and operational opportunities without structured analysis.

The objective of this project was to use Microsoft SQL Server to clean, explore and analyse transactional sales data, enabling management to make better decisions regarding revenue growth, inventory planning, staffing and overall business performance.

Project Overview

The project follows a structured SQL analytics workflow beginning with database creation and data validation before progressing through exploratory analysis and business-focused querying.

Additional computed columns were created to enhance the analysis, including Revenue, Time Period, Month Name, Day Name and Hour of Day. Aggregate functions, CASE statements, Common Table Expressions (CTEs) and window functions were then used to answer key business questions relating to sales, products, stores and customer purchasing behaviour.

The insights generated through SQL formed the analytical foundation for the interactive Coffee Shop Sales Performance Dashboard developed in Power BI.

SQL Analytics Workflow

1. Database Setup

Created the database, imported the dataset into Microsoft SQL Server and verified that all records were successfully loaded before analysis began.

2. Data Cleaning

Validated the dataset by checking record counts, identifying NULL values and searching for duplicate transaction IDs to ensure data quality.

3. Feature Engineering

Created computed columns including Revenue, Month Name, Day Name, Time Period and Hour of Day to support more detailed business analysis.

4. Exploratory Analysis

Explored transactions, stores, products, pricing and sales behaviour to better understand the dataset before answering business questions.

5. Business Analysis

Used SQL queries to analyse revenue, peak trading hours, store performance, product performance and monthly sales trends.

6. Business Intelligence

Converted raw transactional data into meaningful business insights that support operational improvements and data-driven decision-making.

Key Insights & Business Value

The SQL analysis uncovered several valuable insights into sales performance, customer purchasing behaviour and store operations. These findings provide management with actionable information to support strategic planning and day-to-day decision-making.

  • Total revenue and sales performance were successfully measured.

    Calculating total revenue and average transaction values provided a clear understanding of overall business performance. These metrics enable management to monitor growth, evaluate business health and measure future performance against historical results.

  • Peak trading hours were identified.

    Analysing transactions by hour revealed the busiest trading periods. Understanding when customer demand is highest helps managers optimise staff scheduling, improve customer service and reduce waiting times during peak hours.

  • Monthly sales trends highlighted seasonal demand.

    Comparing sales across different months revealed fluctuations in customer activity throughout the year. These trends support more accurate inventory planning, budgeting and seasonal marketing campaigns.

  • Store performance varied across locations.

    Comparing revenue between store locations made it possible to identify the highest-performing branches while highlighting locations that may require additional operational support or targeted sales initiatives.

  • Product performance differed across categories.

    Ranking products and product categories by revenue helped identify the items that contribute most to sales. This information supports inventory management, pricing decisions and promotional planning.

  • Computed columns enabled deeper business analysis.

    Creating additional fields such as Revenue, Month Name, Day Name, Hour of Day and Time Period allowed the business to analyse sales from multiple perspectives, resulting in more detailed and meaningful reporting.

Business Recommendations

Based on the SQL analysis, the following recommendations could help improve business performance and support data-driven decision-making.

  • Increase staffing levels during identified peak trading hours to improve customer service and reduce waiting times.
  • Maintain sufficient inventory for top-performing products to minimise stock shortages and maximise sales opportunities.
  • Review the performance of lower-performing products and evaluate pricing, marketing or promotional strategies to improve sales.
  • Use monthly sales trends to improve demand forecasting, inventory planning and budgeting throughout the year.
  • Compare operational practices across store locations and apply successful strategies from high-performing stores to branches with lower revenue.
  • Continue using SQL analysis alongside Power BI dashboards to monitor business performance and support evidence-based decision-making.
